Task 3.1

Diagnostic target explorer

A test that answers one question may not answer another. Pick a question, find the tests that fit, and read the limit that comes with each. Based on the WOAH (OIE) Terrestrial Manual chapter on FMD and Jamal and Belsham (2013).

TestWhat it detectsFitWhy

Further reading. Jamal and Belsham (2013), Foot-and-mouth disease: past, present and future. Vet Res 44:116. WOAH Terrestrial Manual, chapter 2.1.5, Foot and mouth disease (PDF).

Key idea

Screening or confirmation?

Screening: prioritise sensitivity. Confirmation: prioritise specificity. Match the test to the purpose and to the consequences of error.

ScreeningConfirmation
QuestionWhich animals or herds might be infected?Is this positive result really infection?
PriorityHigh sensitivityHigh specificity
Error to avoidFalse negative: an infected animal is missed and the virus keeps spreading.False positive: a healthy herd is culled, movement is restricted or trade is lost.
How to read the resultA negative result is reliable. It rules infection out.A positive result is reliable. It rules infection in.
FMD exampleScreening many herds in a surveillance programme or testing imported animals.Testing a positive animal again before declaring an outbreak or a free-zone breach.
What you acceptSome false positives, which the next test will sort out.Some missed cases, which screening is meant to catch.

Use two steps. Screen with a sensitive test, then retest every positive with a specific test. When prevalence is low, even a good test gives many false positives (see 1.6), so a positive screening result alone is not enough to act on.

Task 3.3

Same test, different cut-off for a different purpose

The Terrestrial Manual lists four purposes for FMD serology: certify individual animals for trade, confirm suspected cases, substantiate absence of infection, and demonstrate vaccine efficacy. Cut-offs may differ by purpose.

TestIndividual animal, trade certificateHerd-based serosurvey
LPBE (liquid-phase blocking ELISA)Titre below 1/40 negative. Above 1/40 but below 1/90 doubtful: request a second sample, positive if it reaches 1/40.Cut-off 1/90 may be appropriate.
VNT (virus neutralisation)Below 1/16 negative. 1/16 to 1/32 doubtful: request a second sample, positive if it reaches 1/16.Cut-off 1/45 may be appropriate.
SPCE (solid-phase competition ELISA)For maximum sensitivity an inconclusive range of 40–60% inhibition may be set.Above 60% inhibition positive to show freedom in a naive population (serotype O, Pirbright).
rRT-PCRExample from the OIE Reference Laboratory at Pirbright: Ct below 40 positive, 40–50 borderline and retest, above 50 negative. Laboratories define their own cut-offs with reference material.
Why would a serum be "doubtful" for an export certificate but simply counted in a survey?

A certificate speaks for one animal, so a wrong call has a direct trade and disease-spread cost. The programme can afford a second sample. A survey draws conclusions about a herd from many animals, so a single borderline titre matters less. The purpose sets the acceptable error, and the cut-off follows.
