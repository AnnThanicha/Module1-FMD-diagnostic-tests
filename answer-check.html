Test agreement and kappa

Use your 2×2 table from Exercise 2.1. Enter percent with one decimal and kappa with two decimals, choose an interpretation, then press Check answers.

Show the working

Observed agreement = (20 + 11) ÷ 32 = 31 ÷ 32 = 96.9%

Expected = [(20 × 21) + (12 × 11)] ÷ 32² = (420 + 132) ÷ 1,024 = 53.9%

Kappa = (0.969 − 0.539) ÷ (1 − 0.539) = 0.93

A kappa of 0.93 is almost perfect agreement on the usual scale (Landis and Koch). The authors call it excellent. Kappa measures agreement between two tests. It does not say how many infected animals the strip finds, and with only 32 sera it is imprecise.

Exercise 4.1

Case: Has FMD spread to East Java?

Design prevalence 1%, confidence 95%, random sampling from a large population. Answer the four questions, then press Check.

1. Which test would you choose?

Show the reasoning

Some cattle may have been vaccinated, so the test must not react to vaccination. Antibodies to structural proteins (SP) are produced by vaccine and by infection, so an SP test cannot separate them.

NSP antibody tests (3ABC ELISA) are the usual DIVA choice. They detect antibody to non-structural proteins, which appear after infection, not after a purified vaccine. They also find animals that were infected earlier, even when the virus has gone.

RT-PCR is not a good choice here. It detects viral RNA, so it only finds an animal while it carries the virus. We are not testing an active case yet. We are looking for evidence of spread, and a negative RT-PCR on an animal that has cleared the virus would miss it.

2. What is the sensitivity of your test?

Answer: 97.75%

Your answer can be different. The sensitivity depends on the test you chose and on the study you found. Use this answer as a guide.

Explanation

Seeyo et al. (2024), Scientific Reports compared six commercial NSP ELISA kits in Thailand, using 800 serum samples from cattle, buffaloes, pigs and goats. The sensitivity of the kits ranged from 97.75% to 99.50%, and the specificity from 97.25% to 100%. The VDPro kit had the lowest sensitivity, 97.75%.

This answer check uses 97.75%. The value comes from the paper above, and other kits and studies give different values.

If you chose a different test, your value comes from your own source. Give the value, the source and the animals tested in your presentation.

3. How many animals must be sampled?

Answer: 305 animals

Your answer can be different. The number of animals depends on the sensitivity of your test: a more sensitive test needs fewer animals, and a less sensitive test needs more. Use this answer as a guide and follow the same steps with your own sensitivity.

Explanation

DP × Se = 0.01 × 0.9775 = 0.009775

n = ln(1 − 0.95) ÷ ln(1 − 0.009775) = −2.996 ÷ −0.009823 = 305.0, so n = 305

4. All sampled animals test negative. What can you say?

Show the reasoning

You can say: if at least 1% of the animals were infected, this survey would have found at least one positive with 95% probability. All negative means infection at or above 1% is unlikely.

You cannot say: that there is no infection at all. A few infected animals could remain below 1%.

Limits to mention: the sample must be random and cover the whole region. The 97.75% sensitivity comes from one study and is the lowest of the six kits it tested. Sensitivity can differ in other animal populations, so some infected animals could still be missed. Repeat the survey over time to build confidence.

Exercise 5.1

Case: Confirming FMD in suspected pigs

You might choose different tests, with different sensitivity and specificity. Your answers can be different from the ones below. Use this answer as a guide.

1. Which two diagnostic tests will you choose? Why?

Answer: Test A is a virus (antigen or RNA) detection test, such as real-time RT-PCR or an antigen-capture ELISA, on epithelium or vesicular fluid. Test B is an antibody test on serum, such as an NSP ELISA.

Explanation

A suspected case can be at different stages of infection. Early in the disease, an animal can shed a lot of virus but has not made antibodies yet. Later, the virus is clearing but antibodies are high. A virus test and an antibody test together cover both stages, so the infection window is not missed.

Take several sample types: vesicular fluid and epithelium for Test A, and blood for serum for Test B. If the animals may have been vaccinated, use an NSP antibody test, because it does not react to vaccination. An SP antibody test (SPCE or LPBE) is an option in unvaccinated animals.

2. Parallel or serial? Why?

Answer: parallel testing (OR rule).

Explanation

For a suspected case, the priority is high sensitivity and fast containment. A false negative could let FMD spread to other farms and regions.

  • Maximises sensitivity. If either test is positive, the animal is treated as positive.
  • Covers the diagnostic window. The virus test and the antibody test are done at the same time, so an early or late infection is not missed.
  • Saves time. Waiting for a serial sequence, where Test 2 follows Test 1 after days, delays quarantine and control measures.

Decision rule: if Test A is positive or Test B is positive (or both), the case is confirmed positive. Start disease control measures straight away and notify the veterinary authority and WOAH. The case is negative only if both tests are negative and the clinical signs are explained by another disease, for example swine vesicular disease or vesicular stomatitis.

3. What are the sensitivity and specificity of the combined tests?

Answer: sensitivity 99.7%, specificity 97.45%.

Explanation

Test A (RT-PCR): Se = 91.9%, Sp = 100%

Test B (NSP ELISA): Se = 95.8%, Sp = 97.45%

Parallel Se = 1 − (1 − SeA) × (1 − SeB) = 1 − (0.081 × 0.042) = 1 − 0.0034 = 99.7%

Parallel Sp = SpA × SpB = 1.00 × 0.9745 = 97.45%

Parallel testing raises sensitivity above either test alone (91.9% and 95.8%). Specificity stays at 97.45%, as the less specific test sets it. The RT-PCR specificity is 100%, so it adds no false positives.

For comparison, serial testing would give Se = 0.919 × 0.958 = 88.0%. That would miss more infected pigs. These formulas assume the two tests make errors independently. Your numbers can differ if you chose other tests. Give your sources in the presentation.
